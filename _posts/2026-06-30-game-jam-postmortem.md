Participating in the Department Game Jam as a freshman was a challenging but incredibly valuable experience. Our team developed a 2D adventure game, and instead of writing every system from scratch, we decided to aggressively utilize **Harness Engineering**—leveraging AI tools and no-code visual scripting to rapidly prototype mechanics.

Here are the two biggest takeaways from the experience:

### 1. The Power of Fast Prototyping

Time is the most critical resource in a game jam. By offloading boilerplate logic to no-code tools, we had the luxury to focus heavily on the narrative and the core flow of the game. It allowed us to quickly see our ideas come to life and iterate on the gameplay loop much faster than traditional coding would have allowed.

### 2. The Necessity of Strict Code Inspection

While Harness Engineering gave us speed, it also brought significant architectural challenges. When you stitch together generated scripts and visual nodes without a solid foundational design, the logic can easily become entangled. 

We quickly learned that rapid prototyping is not an excuse to skip software engineering fundamentals. As bugs started to emerge from the black-box no-code modules, we realized the critical importance of strict code inspection and maintaining a clear state architecture. 

**Conclusion:** 
The game jam taught me that modern development isn't just about writing code—it's about choosing the right tools to accelerate the process while maintaining the engineering discipline needed to keep the system stable.
