CodingLab: Deep Learning Theory

Founded a high school programming club and designed a deep learning theory curriculum for students with no prior ML background. Delivered approximately one semester of lectures.

Instructor: JeongBeom Baek (Eric)

Term: Spring–Summer

Location: High School, South Korea

Time: Weekly club sessions

Overview

In 2025, after my high school’s existing programming club dissolved, I founded CodingLab (코딩랩) to fill the gap. Rather than teaching basic coding, I designed a deep learning theory curriculum aimed at students with zero ML background.

Source Materials

The curriculum drew from:

  • MIT 6.S191 — Introduction to Deep Learning
  • Hands-On Machine Learning with Scikit-Learn, Keras & TensorFlow by Aurélien Géron
  • Additional papers and online lectures I had independently accumulated

Teaching Philosophy

I restructured graduate-level material into digestible weekly sessions. The challenge was not just knowing the content, but figuring out how to transmit it — finding the right analogies, the right sequence, the right level of abstraction.

Designing this curriculum made me a better engineer. If I couldn’t explain a concept simply, I didn’t fully understand it.

Connection to Current Work

This teaching experience directly informed my current role as a Teaching Assistant under Prof. Hyeji Yang at Sungkyul University, and shaped how I communicate complex technical ideas — whether in a classroom, a game jam presentation, or a hallway conversation with a department head.

Schedule

Week Date Topic Materials
1 Mar What Is AI? — From Rule-Based Systems to Neural Networks

Historical overview of AI, the Turing test, and why deep learning changed the game.
2 Mar Linear Algebra & Calculus Essentials for ML

Vectors, matrices, dot products, and the chain rule — the minimum mathematical foundation for understanding neural networks.
3 Apr Perceptrons and the XOR Problem

Single-layer perceptrons, activation functions, and why XOR broke the first wave of neural networks.
4 Apr Multilayer Networks & Backpropagation

Forward pass, loss functions, gradient descent, and backpropagation — based on MIT 6.S191 materials.
5 May Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)

Convolution, pooling, feature maps, and image classification — drawing from Hands-On ML with Scikit-Learn, Keras & TensorFlow.
6 May Recurrent Neural Networks & Sequence Models

RNNs, LSTMs, and why sequence matters — text generation and time series basics.
7 Jun Modern Architectures & Where the Field Is Going

Transformers, attention mechanisms, and the landscape of LLMs. Open discussion on research directions.
