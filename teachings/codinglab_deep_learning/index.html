CodingLab: Deep Learning Theory & Curriculum

Re-founded a high school programming club (CodingLab) and delivered a deep learning theory curriculum for students with no prior ML background.

🎯 Course Overview

In my sophomore year of high school (2024), when the school’s existing programming club dissolved, I stepped up to recreate and found CodingLab (코딩랩) to ensure the continuity of our community. I served as the Lead Instructor & President.

Goal

To demystify deep learning for high school students by teaching the mathematical foundations and core architectures of modern AI, moving beyond simple API calls to understanding how and why neural networks learn.

Audience

High school students with basic Python knowledge but no prior machine learning or advanced calculus background.

📚 Curriculum Structure

I designed a 7-week intensive curriculum, drawing inspiration from MIT 6.S191 and adapting complex academic concepts into digestible lectures using high school-level mathematics.

Week 1-2: Foundations of Machine Learning

  • The Concept of Learning: What does it mean for a machine to “learn”? (Weights, Biases, and Loss Functions).
  • The Perceptron: The mathematical model of a single neuron.
  • Activation Functions: Why non-linearity is crucial (Sigmoid, ReLU).

Week 3-4: Training Neural Networks

  • Forward Propagation: Matrix multiplication and data flow.
  • Gradient Descent: Navigating the loss landscape.
  • Backpropagation: Deriving the chain rule using partial derivatives to update weights. (I taught students how to manually calculate updates for a 2-layer network).

Week 5: Computer Vision

  • Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN): Feature extraction, kernels, strides, and pooling.
  • Architecture Case Study: LeNet and ResNet.

Week 6: Sequence Modeling

  • Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN): Handling sequential data and time-series.
  • The Vanishing Gradient Problem: Why basic RNNs fail and the intuition behind LSTMs/GRUs.

Week 7: Modern AI Architecture

  • Transformers & Attention: The “Attention is All You Need” mechanism.
  • Large Language Models (LLMs): How models like ChatGPT and Llama are trained and why they hallucinate.

🛠️ Teaching Methodology

  1. Math-First Intuition: Instead of immediately using PyTorch or TensorFlow, I taught the underlying math. We analyzed output probability distributions and manually traced backpropagation equations before writing any framework code.
  2. Visual Learning: Utilized GeoGebra for complex mathematical visualizations and 3D loss landscapes.
  3. Comprehensive Materials: Authored over 80+ slides of original lecture materials containing equations, diagrams, and code snippets.

📈 Impact & Reflection

This experience solidified my belief in “Teaching as Engineering”. Structuring a curriculum is identical to architecting a system: you define the inputs (student baseline), the desired outputs (understanding of DL), and the optimal pathway (the curriculum) to achieve it.

Through Teaching Assistant (TA) roles later in university, I have continued to refine my ability to communicate complex technical concepts clearly, realizing that the ultimate test of understanding is the ability to explain it to someone else.
