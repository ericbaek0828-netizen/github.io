Timeline

A chronological view of the moments that shaped who I am and what I’m building.

2016 — The Spark

AlphaGo vs. Lee Sedol. In middle school, I watched a documentary summarizing the historic 2016 match. Felt a jolt that quietly rewired what I thought was possible. Started pulling on the AI thread and haven’t let go since.

2016–2023 — Self-Directed Learning

While peers followed the standard curriculum, I was independently absorbing AI theory — patching together papers, textbooks, and online lectures. Built a foundation in machine learning, neural networks, and systems thinking before anyone around me knew what a transformer was.

2024 — KSEF: First Research Attempt

Korea Science & Technology Competition (한국과학기술경진대회) — Sophomore year of high school.

Noticed that LLMs were linguistically powerful but epistemically fragile. Proposed a Critical Thinking Framework to address reasoning gaps. Independently arrived at the intuition behind adversarial training loops — years before I had the vocabulary to name it.

Didn’t win. But the problem I identified was real, and the direction I pointed toward became mainstream.

Lesson learned: The gap between a correct intuition and a publishable result is methodology and measurement.

2025 — CodingLab: Teaching as Engineering

When the school’s programming club dissolved, I stepped up to recreate and found CodingLab (코딩랩). Designed a deep learning theory curriculum from MIT 6.S191 and Hands-On ML materials. Delivered approximately one semester of lectures to students with no prior ML background.

Teaching revealed exactly where my understanding ended — a more useful signal than any test score.

2026, March — University Begins

Enrolled at Sungkyul University, Computer Engineering. First semester GPA: 3.97 / 4.5.

Immediately began working as Research & Teaching Assistant under Prof. Hyeji Yang. Recommended by Prof. Yang for the university’s curriculum development initiative.

2026, June — J.A.R.V.I.S. Takes Shape

Began conceptualizing and planning J.A.R.V.I.S., a multi-agent orchestration architecture. Focused heavily on designing layered agent routing, state management, and the Nunchi social context engine.

Published the J.A.R.V.I.S. architecture canvas on this site.

2026, June–July — Game Jam: High Commendation

Department Game Jam (4 days, 3-person team). Built Escape from Uniformity — a 2D side-scrolling adventure with suspicion gauge, stat-based NPC persuasion, and multi-ending structure.

Used Harness Engineering (AI-assisted no-code workflow) to overcome the 2-developer constraint. Received highly positive verbal feedback from judges for our development execution.

During the event, I discussed the Humor Weight Algorithm concept with Prof. Seong-jun Park (Department Head), leading to an encouraging and positive discussion on potential human-robot emotional interaction exploration.

Now — What’s Next

  • Formal research collaboration with Prof. Park on Nunchi-based human-robot interaction
  • Curriculum development initiative at Sungkyul University
  • J.A.R.V.I.S. continues to evolve — the architecture keeps scaling
  • Building toward publishable results with proper methodology
